# SC301 Mock Exam Paper — Pre-Exam Practice

**Module:** SC301 Rethinking Modernity (full year)
**Format:** Open-book, 3 hours, handwritten
**Instructions:** Answer **THREE** questions in total. **At least ONE** from **Section A** (Autumn term, Lectures 1-10). **At least ONE** from **Section B** (Spring term, Lectures 11-17). The third question may come from either section.

Each question carries equal weight. Word target per essay: roughly 700-800 words, handwritten (~5 sides of A4 per essay, ~50 minutes per essay including planning).

**Question types are tagged after each question:**
- 🔁 **Past-Q verbatim** — exactly the wording of a past-year exam question. High recurrence risk; you may see this or a close paraphrase on the real paper.
- 🔂 **Past-Q variant** — close paraphrase or merger of past-year questions.
- ✨ **Fresh** — new question modelled on the lecturer's style; tests whether you can adapt the same material to a new framing.

### Step 2: Open the lecture's notes file

Two ways:

1. **`SC301_ALL_LECTURES.md`** — the merged master. Use Ctrl+F / your reader's search to jump straight to the relevant `# L<N>` heading. The table of contents at the top has anchor links if your reader supports them.
2. **The per-lecture file `LXX_*.md`** — go straight there if you know the lecture number.

### Step 3: Find the closest existing essay

Inside each lecture file, the essays live in §8 (or §9/§10 in some files). The order is **past-Q essays first** (reverse-chronological), then **mock essays** (M1 through M22 or so). Three strategies:

1. **Direct hit:** if the exam question matches a past-Q heading verbatim, that essay is a near-ready answer. Adapt the first paragraph to match the question's exact wording, keep the body.
2. **Close paraphrase:** if the exam question matches a mock-Q (M1-M24), use that essay as your scaffold. Slide-recipe mocks (the first 7 of each lecture's bank) are the closest to lecturer wording.
3. **Combine two essays:** if the exam question merges two ideas (e.g., Foucault on discipline AND biopower in one question), draw paragraph 2-3 from one essay and paragraph 4-5 from another.

### Step 4: Search by concept, not by question

If the question is fresh, search by the named technical concept it uses:

- "the gaze" → L10
- "double consciousness" → L8
- "situated knowledge" → L6 and L10
- "hegemony" → L17
- "culture industry" → L12
- "public sphere" → L13
- "structures of feeling" → L14
- "Black Atlantic" → L8
- "myth" / "denotation/connotation" → L15
- "subaltern counterpublics" → L13
- "war of position" → L17

### Step 5: Use cross-links

Many concepts appear in more than one lecture. The essays are voice-fixed so they read as your own argument, but the cross-links are still useful for evidence:

- Foucault (L7) appears in essays on the gaze (L10) and biopower (L8)
- Du Bois (L8) appears in essays on double consciousness as standpoint (L6) and on the language of race (L4)
- Hall (L9, L15, L17) appears across postcolonial, encoding/decoding, and hegemony
- Williams (L14) appears in mass culture (L11) and culture industry critiques (L12)

### Step 6: Time budget

For each essay:

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-5 min | Read the question carefully. Underline the verb (discuss, critically analyse, to what extent, etc.) and the key concepts. |
| 5-10 min | Find the closest existing essay in your notes. Skim its opening paragraph for the thesis. |
| 10-12 min | On a blank exam page, write your own three-line plan: thesis + 3 body moves + counter-move + conclusion. |
| 12-55 min | Write the essay. Aim for 700-800 words (~5 sides of A4 handwritten). |
| 55-60 min | Read the final paragraph; check you have actually answered the question; sign off. |

### Step 7: Quote sparingly, attribute always

The notes contain many direct quotes with page numbers. Use **one or two** verbatim quotes per essay, no more, ideally where the original phrasing crystallises your argument. The exam is open-book but the markers want to see your own thinking, not transcribed paragraphs from a reader.

### Step 8: When you have time at the end

Do not over-write. If you finish an essay at 50 minutes, do not pad it. Instead, **add one concrete contemporary example** (Coronation Street, drill, Bake Off, Brexit, TikTok algorithm, Black Lives Matter, *Black Mirror*, etc.) to strengthen the closing paragraph. Examples are what markers reward; padding is what markers penalise.
